\section{Problem and Metric}
\label{sec:motivation}

Let an iteration have token budget $B$, prefill bound $C$, and $D_t$ active
decode tokens. A decode-first mixed scheduler can admit prefill work
$P_t\leq\min(C,B-D_t)$. Thus $C$ bounds a chunk, but the current decode batch
implicitly determines the prefill service left in that iteration. PD-TDM
separates the controls: a prefill iteration admits at most
$P_t\leq\min(C,B)$ prompt tokens, while a phase share determines how frequently
prefill is selected when both queues contain work.

We evaluate this exchange with request-level joint-SLO attainment and goodput.
For requests arriving in measurement window $T$, output length $o_i$, and
thresholds $S_T,S_P$, we use
\begin{equation}
G=\frac{1}{T}\sum_i o_i\,
\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{TTFT}_i<S_T\land\mathrm{TPOT}_i<S_P].
\end{equation}
Here TPOT is the per-request mean inter-token time, not a bound on every token
interval. Goodput can differ at similar raw throughput when latency
distributions fall on different sides of the joint threshold.
